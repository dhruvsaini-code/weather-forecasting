End-to-End Architecture

How Phase 3 works

Extending Phase 1's exploratory proofs and Phase 2's state-space models with regularized second-order gradient boosting and chronological cross-validation.

01

Zero-Leakage Pipeline

Chronological partitioning at 18 March 2025 is performed prior to any missing-value handling (0 NaNs in train/test; causal forward-only .ffill()). Rolling windows apply .shift(1) first so day t is never touched.

Perturbation Check: Δx_t(y_t + 1000°C) = 0.000000
02

5-Fold TimeSeriesSplit

Standard random k-fold CV leaks future years into past folds. We tuned n_estimators, max_depth, learning_rate, subsample, and colsample_bytree across five expanding 365-day validation folds.

depth=4, lr=0.025, trees=450 → CV 0.974 °C

✓ Verified: 12 Slides • 10–12 Min Viva
Autoregressive ML features (lag_1, roll_mean_7) observe actual temperatures up to day t - 1 (a Rolling 1-Day-Ahead forecast), whereas Phase 2 Holt-Winters Damped projects all 365 days from 18 March 2025 (a Fixed 365-Day Multi-Step forecast). To ensure a 100% fair comparison, we evaluate both regimes on an equal footing:

• In the Rolling 1-Day-Ahead Regime (h = 1): We compare Tuned XGBoost (1.111 °C RMSE, R² = 0.9744) against Rolling 1-Step SARIMAX (1.177 °C), Rolling 1-Step ARIMA(2,1,2) (1.219 °C), and Rolling 1-Step Naive (1.243 °C). XGBoost wins across every metric.
• In the Fixed 365-Day Multi-Step Regime (h = 1..365, Zero Test Observations): We evaluate XGBoost (Direct 365d — Seasonal + Lag-365 Only) (2.305 °C RMSE) and XGBoost (Recursive 365d Rollout) (3.388 °C). Here, state-space Holt-Winters Damped (2.129 °C) is the Fixed 365-Day Champion, while Direct 365d XGBoost (2.305 °C) beats Fixed 365d SARIMAX (2.376 °C) and Seasonal Naive (2.909 °C).
Three strict safeguards enforce zero lookahead leakage:
1. Post-Split Causal Imputation: We split the series at 18 March 2025 before any imputation, audit missingness (0 NaNs in both train and test), and apply forward-only .ffill() (never pre-split .interpolate() and never backward .bfill()).
2. 1-Day Shifted Rolling Windows: Every rolling mean and standard deviation applies df['tmean'].shift(1).rolling(w) so target day t is excluded.
3. Training-Only Climatology & Perturbation Proof: Day-of-year climatology (clim_doy_mean) is aggregated strictly on the 10,670 training days. Perturbing future temperatures y_t .. y_T by +1000 °C changes the feature vector x_t by exactly 0.000000.
Using 5-fold expanding-window TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=5, test_size=365) inside the training set, deeper trees (max_depth = 6 or 8 with learning_rate = 0.30, CV RMSE 1.0493 °C) overfit to isolated historical weather noise. Restricting tree depth to max_depth = 4 with a slow shrinkage rate (0.025), 450 boosting rounds, and stochastic subsampling (subsample = 0.85, colsample_bytree = 0.80) achieved the lowest 5-fold validation RMSE (0.9736 °C, a 7.2% CV improvement) and generalized best on the 365-day test holdout (1.111 °C).
By total squared-error Gain, the top features are lag_1 (55.7%), roll_mean_7 (17.9%), clim_doy_mean (11.7%), and lag_2 (9.6%), while by split frequency lag_1 (17.3%), lag1_dtr (11.0%), temp_momentum_1d (10.2%), and lag1_humidity (5.8%) dominate tree decisions. The model performs best in calm Post-monsoon (0.918 °C RMSE) and Winter (0.974 °C), and struggles most during sudden Summer dust storms (Andhi, Summer RMSE 1.409 °C) where unannounced convective downdrafts cause a 4–6 °C single-day temperature drop, inducing a 1-day reactive lag spike before correcting the next day.
