Design Synchronization

How we chose

Every modeling decision follows directly from the statistical properties established in Phase 1.

01

Read the seasons

Phase 1 STL decomposition proved that Delhi's annual solar cycle dominates variance (seasonal strength F_s = 0.936) while long-term secular trend is weak (F_t = 0.082).

F_s = 0.936  |  Annual Swing = 19.94 °C
02

Damp the trend

Holt-Winters with an autoregressive damping factor (ϕ = 0.9891) prevents multi-month linear runaway drift, cutting forecast bias from +0.62 °C down to +0.36 °C.

ŷ_{t+h|t} = l_t + Σ ϕ^i b_t + s_{t+h-365}
03

Check the errors

Out-of-sample residuals center near zero, follow a Gaussian bell curve (Jarque-Bera p = 0.163), and innovation errors pass the Ljung-Box white-noise test (p = 0.790).

e_t ~ N(0.36, 2.10²)  |  LB p = 0.790 > 0.05
ADF Unit-Root Test (Raw) t = -10.46 p = 1.35e-18 (Stationary)
KPSS Level Stationarity stat = 0.0232 p ≥ 0.10 (Level Stationary)
Jarque-Bera Normality JB = 3.63 p = 0.163 (Gaussian Fit)
Ljung-Box White Noise Q(14) = 9.61 p = 0.790 (No Autocorrelation)

Operational Value

Who it helps

Translating a 2.13 °C classical forecast benchmark into municipal and infrastructure decisions for Delhi NCR.

Summer MAE: 1.94 °C • RMSE: 2.50 °C

Power grid

Summer demand in Delhi passes 8,000 MW from air-conditioning load. A summer MAE of 1.94 °C lets BSES and Tata Power reserve cooling capacity within a 5% operational buffer.

Heatwave (≥38 °C) MAE: 1.67 °C (45d)

Public health

Across 45 extreme heatwave days (Tmax ≥ 38 °C), the model stays within 1.67 °C MAE, supporting early yellow and orange heat-action advisories.

Cold Snap (≤8 °C) MAE: 2.03 °C (20d)

Winter planning

Across 20 severe cold-wave days (Tmin ≤ 8 °C), error holds near 2.03 °C MAE, aiding night-shelter capacity planning and smog-inversion alerts.
