Methodological Note — 95% Prediction Interval (PI) & Residual Sign Convention: The shaded band is an approximate Gaussian 95% Prediction Interval around future observations (ŷt ± 1.96 σresid), which represents uncertainty around individual future daily temperature realizations yt rather than a confidence interval around a parameter estimate. 02

Stationarity & Damping

ADF vs. KPSS Null Hypotheses: The ADF test (H0: series has a unit root) is rejected (\(p = 1.35 imes 10^{-18}\)), while the KPSS test (H0: series is level-stationary) is not rejected (p ≥ 0.10). Together they support level stationarity around the annual cycle without claiming every aspect of the series is invariant. In Holt-Winters Damped, ϕ = 0.9891 is close to 1, producing gradual trend damping rather than aggressively eliminating the trend.

Critical Scientific Reflection • 6 Key Project Limitations & Future Extensions

Limitations & future work

A transparent assessment of geographical scope, horizon trade-offs, external weather telemetry, recursive error propagation, seasonal differences, and non-stationary climate drift.

Limitation 01 • Spatial Scope

Limited Geographical Coverage

The project focuses on Delhi (1996–2026). It does not automatically generalize to coastal, high-altitude, or humid-tropical cities such as Mumbai, Bengaluru, or Chennai.

→ Improvement: Train and validate models using datasets from additional cities across climatic zones.
Limitation 02 • Forecast Horizon

Long-Term Forecasting Uncertainty

Predicting 365 days ahead (Holt-Winters Damped 2.129 °C, XGBoost Direct Seasonal 2.305 °C) is much harder than predicting tomorrow's temperature (1.111 °C); weather conditions change unpredictably over long horizons.

→ Improvement: Evaluate multiple forecast horizons (e.g., 7, 30, 90, and 365 days) and combine seasonal baselines with short-term updates.
Limitation 03 • Exogenous Telemetry

Limited External Weather Information

Although Phase 3 includes lagged humidity, rainfall, and diurnal temperature range, the model still relies on historical station patterns and does not have a full Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) system with future upper-air atmospheric conditions.

→ Improvement: Integrate wind speed, atmospheric pressure, and cloud cover where reliable data is available.
Limitation 04 • Multi-Step Rollout

Recursive Error Accumulation

In XGBoost Recursive, previous predictions become inputs for subsequent predictions (`lag_1..30` and rolling stats), so early errors compound over 365 steps (raising RMSE to 3.388 °C).

→ Improvement: Compare recursive forecasts with Direct Seasonal approaches (2.305 °C) across horizons.
Limitation 05 • Regime Volatility

Seasonal Performance Differences

Seasonal evaluation shows that Summer has the highest RMSE (2.50 °C HW Damped / 1.41 °C XGBoost) while Post-monsoon has the lowest (1.70 °C / 0.92 °C), indicating accuracy is not uniform throughout the year.

→ Improvement: Explore season-specific sub-models and convective weather features.
Limitation 06 • Non-Stationary Drift

Historical Patterns May Change

Long-term climate warming (+0.042 °C/year), urbanization, and shifting weather patterns may make older historical relationships less representative of future conditions.

→ Improvement: Regularly retrain models on newer observations with recency weighting.

Section 11 • Long-Range Extrapolation Mechanics & Uncertainty

How Each Model Extrapolates to 2030

A. Holt-Winters Damped (ϕ = 0.9891): Preserves the learned level lT, trend bT, and 365-day additive seasonal states from 18 March 2026. Because 0 < ϕ < 1, the cumulative trend contribution Σ ϕjbT converges asymptotically to [ϕ / (1 − ϕ)]bT rather than drifting unboundedly over 1,474 steps.
B. XGBoost Recursive (25 Causal Features): Predicts day t and feeds ŷt into next-day lags (lag_1..365), shifted rolling means/stds (7, 14, 30d), momentum, and anomaly features, while substituting historical DOY climatological expectations for unavailable future humidity, rainfall, and DTR. Early step deviations feed back into subsequent rolling windows, smoothing out high-frequency extremes over multi-year rollouts.
C. XGBoost Direct Seasonal (8 Seasonal/Calendar Features): Uses future calendar coordinates, Fourier harmonics (K = 2), training-derived DOY climatology, and annual lag_365 without short-term recursive lags. Immune to short-term recursive drift, but bound to historical seasonal cycles.
